1. REFERENCE DATA AND NATIVE DERIVATIVE
One real coordinate r is differentiated at 0. Work over complex finite spaces.
S is differentiable positive Hermitian; H is differentiable Hermitian.
H C = S C E, C^dag S C = I; C is square complete, E real diagonal.
Write A=C^dag H' C, B=C^dag S' C, D=A-BE; row j, column i uses e_i.
A,B are Hermitian. Thus, entrywise in any dimension,
  D-D^dag = EB-BE, or D_ji-conj(D_ij)=(e_j-e_i)B_ji.
  F=(D+D^dag)/2=A-(BE+EB)/2 is Hermitian.
F is an algebraic Hermitian part, not automatically a physical Hamiltonian/force.
D is Hermitian iff [E,B]=0; real-valued examples would conceal conjugation errors.
D is real on its diagonal; within an equal-energy block it is Hermitian.

If a differentiable normalized eigenbranch is available, define K=C^dag S C'.
Differentiating the defining eigenproblem and normalization gives
  A+EK-BE-KE-E'=0,  K+K^dag+B=0.
For nondegenerate eigenvalues, D_ji=(e_i-e_j)K_ji off diagonal,
and e_i'=D_ii; Re K_ii=-B_ii/2, with arbitrary imaginary diagonal phase gauge.
At degeneracy the off-diagonal division is unavailable; do not divide by zero
or infer a unique eigenvector derivative. The adjoint and normalization identities
remain meaningful. An eigenbasis inside a degenerate subspace is not unique.
The generic real-symbol 2x2 block verifies all component identities directly;
the displayed entrywise proof supplies the dimension-independent argument.

The exact asymmetric example uses C=[[1,i],[1+i,2]], E=diag(-2,3),
S=C^-dag C^-1, H=C^-dag E C^-1, with Hermitian A=[[2,1+3i],[1-3i,-1]],
B=[[1,2-i],[2+i,-2]]. These define realizable local H',S' in that C frame;
S stays positive in a sufficiently small neighborhood by continuity. Native
D=[[4,-5+6i],[5-i,5]]. Both B and E fail to commute. The ledger exposes final-
energy substitution, omitted overlap, ordinary rather than S normalization,
transposition instead of adjoint, and the claim that D itself is Hermitian.

2. ENERGY ORIGIN, COMPLEX GAUGE, NORMALIZATION AND COORDINATE FRAMES
A common real shift alpha(r) of the represented operator means
H -> H+alpha S, E -> E+alpha I, H' -> H'+alpha S'+alpha' S.
Consequently D -> D+alpha' I. For a constant shift D is unchanged.
Adding alpha I instead of alpha S to a nonorthogonal coordinate matrix,
omitting alpha S', or reversing its sign is not this energy-origin shift.
A coordinate-dependent origin has a nonzero derivative and is not a constant
origin convention; no invariant-force assertion follows from it.

A reference phase gauge P (unit-modulus diagonal) gives D -> P^dag D P.
Individual complex entries are not invariants; their modulus squares are.
A unitary within a degenerate equal-energy block has the same covariance;
a permutation also permutes the energy labels. Mixing unequal energies while
keeping E diagonal and fixed is not a reference-eigenbasis gauge.
Nonunit column scaling L commuting with E gives unnormalized raw matrix
L^dag D L. Restore S normalization before identifying its entries with D.
The derivative of an eigenvector phase changes K's imaginary diagonal, not
D's fixed-reference definition; complex bra factors must be conjugated.

For a moving coefficient-frame transform Hbar=U^dag H U, Sbar=U^dag S U,
with U(0)=I in the reference S=I, H=E frame and G=U'(0),
  Dbar = D+[E,G].
The product-rule check differentiates the full transformed curves, not an
assumed covariance rule. In particular
  Fbar-F = [E,G-G^dag]/2.
Hermitization alone does not erase a moving-frame connection.

At S(0)=I the symmetric inverse-square-root orthonormalizer has first jet
O(0)=I, O'=-B/2: differentiation of O S O=I with Hermitian O' proves this.
Its coordinate Hamiltonian derivative is F. The block verifies the first jet
with O=I-rB/2 (this polynomial is NOT asserted exactly orthonormal for all r).
Another orthonormalizer O U with unitary U(0)=I,U'=J anti-Hermitian has the
same metric first jet but coordinate derivative F+[E,J]. Such first jets
extend to exact differentiable orthonormalizers locally by S^-1/2 U.
Therefore an orthonormal metric alone does not specify a laboratory frame.
The exact example has a nonzero Hermitian derivative change [E,J].

3. DECLARED EMBEDDING: WHAT SUFFICES, WHAT DOES NOT
Declare full-column-rank T(r) into a FIXED finite ambient Hilbert frame,
S=T^dag T and H=T^dag h T, with h Hermitian. At reference define
  Phi=T C, Y=T' C, Z=Phi^dag Y, V=Phi^dag h' Phi,
  W=h Phi-Phi E.
Then Phi^dag Phi=I and Phi^dag W=0. A generalized eigenproblem implies only
this projected residual, not W=0 in a larger ambient space. Differentiating
products directly yields in all dimensions
  B=Z+Z^dag,
  A=V+Z^dag E+E Z+Y^dag W+W^dag Y,
  D=V+[E,Z]+Y^dag W+W^dag Y.
These follow by substituting h Phi=Phi E+W in the two differentiated T terms.
The rectangular exact counterexample verifies the otherwise easily lost last
two terms. Conjugation and both product-rule terms are essential.

SQUARE INVERTIBLE T: Phi is a complete orthonormal ambient frame, W=0.
  V=D-[E,Z],  F=V+[E,Z-Z^dag]/2.
Hence known T,T' (and C) suffice to obtain Z and V. In this square case,
they also give h'=Phi V Phi^dag in the declared ambient frame. Equivalently
full H,S,H' with T,T' suffice by differentiating h=T^-dag H T^-1.
Knowing D, B and E supplies A=D+BE if that coordinate derivative is needed.
The metric fixes only Z+Z^dag, not its anti-Hermitian part. F=V iff
[E,Z-Z^dag]=0; Z=B/2 is sufficient, not necessary. This connection choice
is extra information. Square-case diagonal and equal-energy blocks of V
are fixed by D even when the inter-energy off-diagonal entries are not.

EXACT SQUARE NONUNIQUENESS, SAME ENTIRE H(r),S(r):
T1=I+r M, M=[[1,2+i],[-1+3i,-2]], h1=E+r V1,
E=diag(-2,3), V1=[[3,-2+4i],[-2-4i,-1]].
J=[[i,1+2i],[-1+2i,-i]] is anti-Hermitian.
U=(I+rJ/2)(I-rJ/2)^-1 is unitary for real r.
T2=U T1, h2=U h1 U^dag. Product cancellation proves identical S,H for
all r where T1 is invertible. The exact ledger verifies the rational curves.
At 0, h1=h2=E and T1=T2=I, but
h1'=V1, h2'=[[3,3+14i],[3-14i,-1]]=V1+[J,E].
The anti-Hermitian connection changes Z2=Z1+J and restores the same D.
Viewed as TWO possible embedded physical families in a common declared fixed
ambient frame, they are indistinguishable from H,S, even all derivatives.
Viewed instead as a passive moving ambient frame, the ordinary derivative
changes and must be replaced by a declared covariant derivative. Calling the
two meanings identical would assume the very frame information under test.
This supplies a constructive nonuniqueness result, not a numerical search.

RECTANGULAR T: known T,T' is not sufficient from H,S alone unless missing
ambient action W (or a condition killing its contraction with Y) is supplied.
If W=0 (h-invariant image), or Y is tangent to the image, the square projected
formula holds; it still does not determine complement blocks of full h'.
With general known ambient h0 and T,T', W is available, and the displayed
formula suffices for projected V. Full ambient h' remains underdetermined
outside the embedded image without more data.

EXACT RECTANGULAR NONUNIQUENESS EVEN WITH SAME ENTIRE T,H,S:
T=[[1,0],[0,1],[r,ir]], n=[-r,ir,1]^T, v=[1+i,2-i,0]^T.
T^dag n=0 exactly. h1=diag(-2+3r,3-r,7),
h2=h1+n v^dag+v n^dag. Both are Hermitian; T^dag h2 T=T^dag h1 T exactly.
At 0, Phi selects the first two ambient coordinates and Z=0.
V1=diag(3,-1), but V2=[[1,-1-2i],[-1+2i,-3]].
For h2, W has only last row [1-i,2+i], and
Y^dag W+W^dag Y=[[2,1+2i],[1-2i,2]]. Thus D=V2+that correction=V1.
Even diagonal hprime matrix elements differ: projecting a moving noninvariant
subspace is not equivalent to differentiating an ambient eigenproblem.
These finite matrices are counterexamples to sufficiency claims, not models
of unrepresented physical orbitals or a force prescription for USPP.
