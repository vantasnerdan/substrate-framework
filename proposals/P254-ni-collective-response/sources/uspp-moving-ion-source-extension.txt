3. QUESTION1: WHAT MOVES, WHAT OVERLAP, WHAT VELOCITY LAW?

[PAPER] Sec.II p.2 Eqs.(1)-(3) sets the generalized eigenproblem H psi_n =
epsilon_n S psi_n and the meaningful inner product <psi_m|S|psi_n>,
corresponding to true-state inner products. S is positive Hermitian and
contains integrated augmentation q_ij^I times beta projectors, not density-
dependent screening. On p.3 Eq.(4) beta_i^I(x)=beta_i(x-X_I); Eqs.(5),(6)
make H's nonlocal coefficients self-consistent with the local, Hartree and
XC potential. Eqs.(7)-(9) distinguish the spatial augmentation Q_ji^I(x)
from its integral q_ij^I. The paragraph before Eq.(10) explicitly says S
depends on ionic positions only, not electronic charge density.

[PAPER] For fixed ions, Eq.(10), p.3, identifies H(t)psi with both
 i*hbar*d_t[S psi] and S[i*hbar*d_t psi].
For moving ions Eq.(11) warns that these differential operators are NOT
equivalent, their difference being proportional to ionic velocity. The
paper does NOT resolve this by choosing either ordering arbitrarily.
It introduces U and T in Eqs.(12)-(18), then gives, p.3:

 Eq.(19): H psi_n = i*hbar*T^dagger[d_t(T psi_n)]
                   = i*hbar*[T^dagger*T*d_t psi_n
                              + T^dagger*(d_t T)*psi_n].
 Eq.(21): P = -i*hbar*T^dagger*(d_t T) = sum_I P^I dot Xdot_I.
 Eq.(23): (H+P) psi_n = i*hbar*S*d_t psi_n.

These are transcriptions of the original, with hats suppressed for plain
text; P^I is a vector operator and Xdot_I the ion velocity. Eq.(20) and
Eq.(22) explicitly differentiate BOTH ket partial-wave differences and
bra projectors with respect to X_I. Keeping only a displaced beta term
would not be the displayed law. The paragraph after Eq.(23) calls this a
moving spatial 'gauge' and states restrictions: pseudopotential cores must
not overlap and core-level electrons must not be excited.

[PAPER] On p.4 Eqs.(25),(26) introduce atom-local t_I with T=1+sum_I t_I
and use rigid translation. Eq.(27) writes the velocity coefficient as
 P^I = -i*hbar*T^dagger*partial_(X_I) t_I
     = (1+t_I^dagger)(1+t_I) p
       - (1+t_I^dagger) p (1+t_I),
where p is ELECTRON momentum. It is not nuclear momentum and it is not a
bath operator. The source explicitly says P^I and P are not Hermitian.
It immediately distinguishes the immobile-ion Eq.(28), H(t)psi_n =
i*hbar*S*d_t psi_n, and defers integration algorithm/error analysis for
coupled ion-electron Eq.(23) to a later paper. That later paper has not
been acquired or used here.

[INFERENCE] The source's answer to the time-dependent metric question is
an explicit moving reconstruction, not 'drop dS/dt', 'use dS/dt alone', or
'make P Hermitian'. No two-time finite-step cross-overlap matrix/export
S(t,t') is specified in this original. Its Eq.(35) norm comparison belongs
to the SAME fixed S in Sec.III. Neither that norm formula nor its fixed-S
propagator is a validated moving-ion discretization for installed GBRV.
No new norm/connection identity is derived in this report.

[PAPER] Frozen-ion limits are explicit, not inferred from the examples:
Sec.III's title and opening on p.4 restrict it to immobile or approximately
immobile ions. Eqs.(29),(30) rewrite that fixed-S problem; the T-hat in
Eq.(30) denotes TIME ORDERING, not the PAW reconstruction of Eq.(14).
Eqs.(36)-(38), p.5, give the Crank-Nicolson step using H(t); the text calls
it first-order when H changes, second-order in the special time-invariant
H limit, including an infinitesimal test electron that does not perturb H.
Eqs.(39),(40) are a predictor-corrector for changing electronic density,
STILL within the immobile-ion section.

[PAPER] Sec.IV pp.5-6 applies electronic impulses to Na2 and benzene at
specified geometries, with charge/potential updates, not ionic dynamics.
It explicitly skips the mapped impulse Eq.(47): pseudo rather than true
wavefunctions are used in Eq.(41), acknowledged to make oscillator
strengths not exact and slightly violate the sum rule. This is a stated
benchmark approximation, not an approved prescription for omitting T.

explicitly calls the conductance demonstration a 'fixed band approximation'

examples do not exercise Eq.(23)'s moving-ion connection or nuclear heating.

4. QUESTION2: A PARTICULAR EMBEDDING OR AN ARBITRARY HERMITIZATION?

[PAPER] Eqs.(12),(13), p.3, first expose unitary freedom in splitting S.
The paragraph introducing Eq.(14) then selects U by referring to the PAW
transformation, and explicitly writes
 T = U^dagger S^(1/2)
   = 1 + sum_(i,I) (|tilde_psi_i^I>-|psi_i^I>)<beta_i^I|,
 tilde_psi_n = T psi_n.
Here tilde means the true nodal wavefunction, opposite to some other
papers' notation (p.2). The channel i in the sum is not a saved crystal
band n. Eq.(15) identifies a transformed true Hamiltonian with core-level
electrons frozen; Eqs.(16)-(19) invoke its all-electron TDDFT propagation
and transform it back. This is the paper's asserted physical identification
under its PAW/USPP assumptions, not a new verification of that identification
for every possible UPF or finite plane-wave/band truncation.

[INFERENCE] This is real additional source authority for a CONDITIONAL
PAW-based electronic dynamics. It is not the assertion that every S admits
one uniquely physical square-root reconstruction. Setting U=1 would discard
the specific partial-wave map the source supplies; no such choice is made
here. Nor is the paper defining a force by taking the Hermitian part of
native D. Its explicit non-Hermitian P is especially incompatible with
such a replacement as an automatic 'repair'. Non-Hermiticity of this
moving-coordinate term is not evidence of physical damping.

[INFERENCE] At fixed X the source permits a fixed-S orthonormal propagation
representation, Eq.(29). Extending an orthonormalizer to varying X and
identifying its coordinate derivative with a laboratory force is a DIFFERENT
claim. Qian instead supplies a physical-map premise with channel data and
a velocity law. The settled oracle's frame/embedding cautions continue to
apply to transferring this premise into finite exported data. No new map,
connection, effective independent-electron force model or consumer converter
has been selected by this extension.
