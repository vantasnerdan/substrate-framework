# Exact current closure test beyond the first Fourier shell

This is the failure-derived representation change after the first-shell
initial correction was refuted at the second observed time derivative.
No new finite basis is used. The result concerns the declared fixed
two-wave field and planar transverse orientation. It is not a verdict on
whole-law averaged closure, generalized0163, or the parent same-EPS goal.

## 1. The actual measured current has an energy identity

Use psi,alpha,A of velocity-current.md and the unnormalized transverse
kappa=(0,1,1), D=(0,1,-1). Normalization rescales positive constants only.
For an arbitrary smooth, zero-mean, solenoidal first velocity correction,
average in the translation-invariant axial x direction. Its planar part
has streamfunction phi. Set

    H=-Delta, B=H-1.

On the square two-torus H has eigenvalues |q|^2; B>=0 on the mean-zero
space. Its four-dimensional kernel consists of the first-shell sine/cosine
functions. The actual forced planar velocity equation is

    H phi_t=-A B phi+2A alpha.                            (1)

The physical projected current in(4) of the preceding file has a constant
q_D contribution plus

    C(t)=< (0,2sinZ,2sinY).z >=-2<alpha phi>.              (2)

The full pressure was used to obtain(1); taking curl eliminated a gradient,
not a physical velocity component. No additional axial correction can
change(1),(2) for this orientation, since its axial-current coefficient is0.

On the range of B define

    Z=(H B)^(1/2) phi,
    Q=(B/H)^(1/2),
    G=-Q A Q, f=2Q A alpha.                              (3)

The zero modes of B are retained as physical streamfunction components,
but do not enter Z. Projection of(1) gives exactly

    Z_t=G Z+f,
    C_t=-<f,Z>=-d(||Z||^2/2)/dt.                         (4)

The last equality follows from the skewness of A and hence G. It includes
the actual changing first-shell current, not just the range variable.

The range evolution is a unitary group: the conserved quadratic energy
of the unforced equation is <phi,H B phi>, coercive on the complement of
the first shell. Smooth linear Euler evolution exists in both time
directions on this fixed smooth periodic background. Conservation,
uniqueness and completion give the strongly continuous unitary group
generated by the closure of G. This supplies the operator domain; no
spectral gap for transport is postulated. Q is bounded, self-adjoint,
and boundedly invertible on this range because |q|^2>=2 there.

## 2. A current constant on a finite interval would give a range equilibrium

Here f is a finite Fourier function, supported on q=(+/-1,+/-1).
The multiplier Q does not enlarge support. Each application of G shifts
support by one base Fourier wave and differentiates at most once. Thus

    ||G^r f|| <= C^r (r+2)! ||f||.                        (5)

This is an analytic-vector estimate, not a numerical spectral assumption.
Consequently <f,exp(tG)Z0> is real analytic in t for every finite-energy
Z0: move the unitary group to f and use(5). The radius is uniform along
the real time axis by unitarity and commutation with G. The forcing
integral has the same property. Therefore a current constant on any open
time interval has C_t identically0 for all real t.

By(4), ||Z(t)|| is then constant. The affine-unitary trajectory is bounded.
Its Cesaro averages Zbar_T=(1/T)integral_0^T Z(t)dt are bounded and obey

    G Zbar_T+f=(Z(T)-Z(0))/T ->0.

Take a weakly convergent subsequence in this Hilbert space. The graph of
a closed linear operator is weakly closed, so its limit belongs to the
domain and satisfies G Z_*+f=0. The same argument applies to mild solutions
by the integrated-generator identity. Thus a constant actual current
requires a genuine stationary RANGE solution, not stationary material
displacement or even stationary full microscopic velocity.

## 3. Reflection parity rules out that equilibrium

Q commutes with the reflections Y->-Y and Z->-Z. A maps even-even
functions to odd-odd functions and conversely. The forcing f is odd-odd.
The odd-odd component of GZ_*+f=0 therefore gives

    Q A[Q Z_*,even-even-2alpha]=0.

There is no first-shell or constant mode in the odd-odd sector, so Q is
invertible there. Hence

    A[Q Z_*,even-even-2alpha]=0.                          (6)

As in the actual streamline argument in velocity-current.md, the bracket
must be a function of psi almost everywhere. Its pairing with alpha is
therefore0. But Q Z_* is orthogonal to alpha whereas <alpha^2>=1, making
the same pairing equal to-2. Contradiction.

Thus no finite-enstrophy initial correction gives exactly constant physical
current on an open time interval for this fixed field and transverse
orientation. Smooth corrections are included. This is an actual observation-
level mechanism; it did not demand stationary chi or invert the full Euler
operator on an arbitrary function space.

## Scope and positive continuation

The first-shell algebra was a diagnostic; equations(1)--(6) now explain its
failure across the complete finite-energy planar-current class. They do
not require every realization of an isotropic ensemble to have a separately
constant current. For orientations with axial components, passive axial
current can contribute; whole-law phase correlations can also matter.
Those are distinct candidates, not covered by this result.

The positive generalized0163 construction remains a concrete alternative
background with actual current correctors and finite-time acoustic control.
It is not transferred to a constant-lambda EPS insertion by assuming a
transport inverse. The immediate next construction is an orientation-law
adjoint-current cancellation retaining the axial passive variable, or a
same-field generalized core/optical join, rather than enlarging a finite
Fourier box after an analytic mechanism has already explained that class.
