# A genuine finite physical stress closure and the EPS insertion interface

This is a representation-level comparison of the known0151 one-wave
source, not a second promotion of that source or an EPS optical claim.
It shows exactly which finite cell structure is lost under insertion.

Let u=v0(e1 cos(lambda n.x)+e2 sin(lambda n.x)), with e1,e2,n orthonormal.
Its pressure is constant. The actual first-cell fields supplied by (4)
depend only on n.x and lie perpendicular to n. On this subspace

    A chi=0, (Du)chi=0, hence C=0 and L_E=0.

Thus the reachable internal operator G is genuinely nilpotent of index2,
on the finite sine/cosine transverse coefficient space. No approximation,
postulated oscillator or frequency averaging makes it finite. Write
P_n=Id-n tensor n. The exact first-cell input/output law is

    chi_tt=-2P_n[a X_t],
    chi_t=-2P_n[aX]+P_n[aD-kappa(u.D)].                 (1)

Its integration constant is the actual Kelvin preparation, not discarded.
Nevertheless its CONTRIBUTION TO THE PHYSICAL STRESS vanishes exactly:

    O_v P_n[aD-kappa(u.D)]=0.                          (2)

To see this, phase covariance is <u tensor u>=s P_n, s=v0²/2.
Put q=n.kappa and e=P_n kappa. Then <a²>=s(1-q²),
<a(u.D)>=-s q(n.D), <u a>=s e, and <u(u.D)>=s P_nD.
Substitution into O_v z=<a z+u(kappa.z)> cancels all four terms in(2).
This is a measured-stress cancellation, not a vanishing internal state.

The resulting physical stress matrix on kappa-transverse X is

    R(X)=-s(1-q²)X
            +2s(1-2q²)(P_kappa n)(n.X).                (3)

Before whole-field averaging this matrix has orientation-dependent signs.
The common-direction Haar moments <q²>=1/3 and
<q²(P_kappa n) tensor(P_kappa n)>=P_kappa/15 give

    E R=-4s X/15=-(2v0²/15)X.                         (4)

Equations(1)--(4) are a finite actual physical stress closure at the
second spatial jet, with its initial preparation explicitly checked.
The inherited action and canonical/measured current of the preceding
files remain valid. This does not extend the whole-law physical mean
approximation to t~1/k, where different orientation speeds can dephase.
