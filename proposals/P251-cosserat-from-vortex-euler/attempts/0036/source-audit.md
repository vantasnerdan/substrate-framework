These are external route inputs, not accepted framework claims. The analytic
construction in this attempt is written out independently; the papers below

## Garcia: polygon patches

[C. Garcia, arXiv:2010.07361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07361), Theorem 1.1,
constructs small co-rotating polygonal arrays of Euler patches. Setting the
number of components to six supplies a finite-area relative equilibrium.
The vorticity is discontinuous at the component boundaries. The theorem is
an existence statement, not a smooth-vorticity or optical-spectrum theorem.
It verifies that a finite-patch hexagon is a genuine candidate, but does not
alone prove the smooth-core action requested here.

## Long--Wang--Zeng: concentrated patches and slow/fast splitting

[Long, Wang, Zeng, arXiv:1809.06425v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06425v3),
Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, treat concentrated steady patches near nondegenerate
point-vortex critical points in bounded planar domains. The spectral theorem
does supply a finite-dimensional slow sector and a separated fast sector,
with quantitative concentration estimates. A rotating whole-plane polygon
has different domain and symmetry hypotheses, so importing the conclusion
requires an actual extension, not deletion of those hypotheses. Theorem 1.5
gives a different continuous-vorticity construction with finite regularity;
it is not a smooth-core spectral-persistence theorem. This motivates the
explicit radial-core estimate and fixed-disk construction here.

## Radu--Stevenson: genuinely smooth desingularization

[Radu, Stevenson, arXiv:2511.18592v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18592v2),
Definition 1.2 and Theorem I, prove infinitely smooth, compact-vorticity
desingularization of admissible rotating patches. Admissibility includes a
simply connected patch, dihedral symmetry, a transverse streamfunction
boundary, and a specific boundary-operator nondegeneracy condition. A union
of six separated patches is not the stated simply connected object. A
multiple-component extension and its coupled boundary invertibility would
need proof. The theorem's velocity convergence is not an operator-norm
convergence statement for the linearized Euler generator and cannot supply
optical spectral persistence by itself. The independent smooth Green-equation
route in this attempt avoids those two unproved transfers.

## Averkiou--Musso: three-dimensional helical polygons

[Averkiou, Musso, arXiv:2511.12296v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12296v1),
Theorem 2, constructs global helical Euler polygon configurations with compact
cross-sectional vorticity. It is a valuable three-dimensional continuation
candidate. The proof uses a positive-part power with exponent greater than
three; Section 3 explicitly states its finite differentiability. Thus its
use of smooth terminology does not establish infinite differentiability for
a fixed exponent. Fixed positive pitch is also a hypothesis; a uniform
infinite-pitch planar limit is not stated. The exact screw symmetry may turn
its translating-rotating solution into a stationary field after an axial
Galilean shift, but neither constant-parameter Beltrami structure nor an
optical action is supplied by this theorem. Parent attempt 0039 handles
the stationary transformation.
