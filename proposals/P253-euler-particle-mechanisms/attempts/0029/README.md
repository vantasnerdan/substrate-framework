## Frozen leading homological problem

Put \(L=|\log\epsilon|\), use source phase \(\phi=\omega Lt\), and on
\(|j|\ge2\) retain the real diagonal frequencies

\[
 \mu_j=c_1j+c_2\operatorname{sign}j+\epsilon^2c_3d_j,
 \qquad d_j=-\frac{j}{4(j^2-1)|j|}.                         \tag{6}
\]

For two independent labels \(a,b\in\{1,2\}\), the nonresonant smoothing
homological equation has Fourier divisor

\[
 \Delta_{\ell jk}
 =\epsilon^2L\omega\ell+\mu_j-\mu_k,
 \qquad
 i\Delta_{\ell jk}X^{ab}_{\ell jk}=R^{ab}_{\ell jk}.       \tag{7}
\]

Equation (7) exposes a concrete obstruction, not a generic warning: the two
labelwise principal spectra are identical, so

\[
 \Delta_{0jj}=0\qquad (a\ne b)                              \tag{8}
\]

for every high mode. Scalar diagonalization is therefore the wrong
representation. The construction must retain each label-degenerate complex
space

\[
 \mathcal B_j=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
 \{e^{ij\theta}\otimes e_1,e^{ij\theta}\otimes e_2\},      \tag{9}
\]

together with its \(-j\) real/symplectic partner, as a finite-dimensional
Krein block. There can be infinitely many such finite-dimensional blocks;
0029 may call only the additional low-mode or accidental clusters finite in
number after proving that fact. The source half-period label relation belongs
to its existence chart and cannot remove (8) from the physical perturbation
space.

Two already exposed source features make the continuation genuinely
constructive. First, all \(\mu_j\) are real, hence the unperturbed diagonal
high-mode generator is skew-adjoint and its return is unitary. Second, for
sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), the source coefficient bounds

\[
 -\frac23\le c_1\le-\frac13,
 \qquad \frac13\le c_2\le\frac{13}{24}                     \tag{10}
\]

separate the autonomous \(\ell=0\) high modes except for the exact same-mode
label degeneracy (8): same-sign differences have the leading gap
\(|c_1||j-k|\), while for opposite signs and \(|j|,|k|\ge2\),
\(|j-k|\ge4\) and the leading cancellation leaves at least
\(4/3-13/12=1/4\), before the explicit \(O(\epsilon^2|d_j-d_k|)\)
correction. After activation this estimate must be written with its exact
small-\(\epsilon\) remainder and source quantifiers. It is a high-mode
stability feature of the diagonal generator, not stability of the actual
monodromy. The unresolved divisors are the time-dependent \(\ell\ne0\) pairs
and the retained label blocks.

## Route A — convergent block reducibility

Construct periodic bounded symplectic maps \(S_m(\phi)\) on the full two-label
Sobolev scale and block-diagonalize the actual generator rather than the
source-restricted boundary-value operator. At iteration \(m\), let
\(\nu^{(m)}_{j,p}\), \(p\in\{1,2\}\), be the eigenfrequencies of the retained
label block \(\mathcal B_j\). Invert only off-block entries satisfying an
explicit second-Melnikov condition of the form

\[
 \left|\epsilon^2L\omega\ell
       +\nu^{(m)}_{j,p}-\nu^{(m)}_{k,q}\right|
 \ge \gamma_m\langle\ell\rangle^{-\tau_t}
                 \langle j-k\rangle^{-\tau_x},             \tag{11}
\]

with exact resonant equivalence classes excluded from inversion. Derive the
needed \(\tau_t,\tau_x\), regularity reserve, and summable sequence
\(\gamma_m\) from the actual smoothing order; they are not free fitted
parameters.

Route A must:

1. calculate \(\partial_\lambda\) of every divisor in (11), splitting
   same-sign, opposite-sign, \(\ell=0\), and \(\ell\ne0\) cases;
2. prove a nonempty, preferably asymptotically full-measure, sub-Cantor set
   inside the actual source set rather than assuming a favorable \(\lambda\);
3. solve the full independent-label homological equations with summable tame
   losses and prove convergence of \(S_m^{\pm1}\) on one declared Sobolev
   space;
4. carry the first-mode/center and conserved-companion blocks through the
   reduction and quotient only the exact space (2);
5. show the limiting finite-step residual vanishes in operator norm or is
   absorbed into the retained blocks, thereby earning rather than assuming
   `CT`; and
6. prove every limiting block is uniformly similar to a unitary block.

**Route-A success:** an exact bounded periodic similarity of
\(M_{\rm red}\) to a uniformly conditioned direct sum of unitary finite-label
blocks, establishing (3).

## Route B — direct positive periodic metric

Without fully diagonalizing, construct a periodic self-adjoint metric
\(G(t):\mathcal N^s_{\rm red}\to(\mathcal N^s_{\rm red})^*\) satisfying

\[
 \dot G+A_{\rm red}(t)^*G+G A_{\rm red}(t)=0,
 \qquad mI\le G(t)\le MI,\qquad 0<m\le M<\infty.          \tag{12}
\]

The construction starts from the diagonal unitary metric but must solve the
metric homological equation against every actual self, cross, first/shape, and
finite-step term. It must be performed on the fixed-level quotient (2), not on
the reversible delayed-label subspace. A formal Hamiltonian Hessian, real
frequencies, or weak KKS conservation does not by itself imply positivity.

Equation (12) gives the exact positive conclusion

\[
 U_{\rm red}(t,0)^*G(t)U_{\rm red}(t,0)=G(0),
 \qquad M_{\rm red}^*G(0)M_{\rm red}=G(0),                 \tag{13}
\]

so \(G(0)^{1/2}M_{\rm red}G(0)^{-1/2}\) is unitary and (3) follows with
condition number at most \((M/m)^{1/2}\).

**Route-B success:** a fully defined metric with the bounds and evolution
identity (12), including uniform control of all retained resonant blocks.

Route B is materially different from Route A: it solves one operator Lyapunov
problem and may tolerate non-diagonal compact structure, whereas Route A
constructs a convergent modal similarity and a new sub-Cantor set.
