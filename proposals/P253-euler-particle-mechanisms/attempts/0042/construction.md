## 3. Route B1: exact causal retained history

equation becomes Euler with the smooth finite-rank forcing

    u_t=-P_L[(u dot grad)u]
       +v_t+Pi P_L[(u dot grad)u].                    (4)

Here `u_0=v(0)+w_0`. The standard Euler transport/energy estimate in
`H^s_sigma`, rather than an `H^s` Banach-ODE argument, applies to (4): the
transport term has the usual one-derivative form, while `v_t` is smooth and

    partial_t Pi(u-v)=Pi B(u,u)-Pi B(u,u)=0,

so `w=u-v` stays in `Q H^s_sigma` and solves the second row of (3). This
constructs `W[v,w_0]`. Substitution gives the exact causal history equation

    v_t(t)=Pi B(v(t)+W[v,w_0](t),v(t)+W[v,w_0](t)).       (5)

## 4. Route B2: the Mori--Zwanzig operator identity and its domain boundary

Let `P_obs` be a bounded linear idempotent on the chosen observable space and
put `Q_obs=1-P_obs`; these are not the velocity projection `Pi,Q`. On a common
invariant domain where `L` and `Q_obs L` generate the needed evolutions and

    e^(tL)=e^(t Q_obs L)
       +integral_0^t e^((t-s)L) P_obs L
          e^(s Q_obs L) ds.                           (6)

Splitting `L A=P_obs L A+Q_obs L A` and applying (6) to the second term gives

    d/dt e^(tL)A=e^(tL)P_obs L A+e^(t Q_obs L)Q_obs L A
       +integral_0^t e^((t-s)L)P_obs L
          e^(s Q_obs L)Q_obs L A ds.                  (7)

The middle term carries unresolved initial data and the last term is memory.
Putting `P_obs` on the whole left side would project the middle term to zero and
is not the trajectory identity.  The activated README was corrected for this

Equation (7) applies componentwise to the complete material vector only when
those observables lie in the common invariant domain. It is exact whenever
both operator evolutions exist. A general infinite-dimensional Euler theorem
