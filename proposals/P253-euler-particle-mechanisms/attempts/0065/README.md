# P253/0065: topological and nonlocal Euler charge routes

## Frozen parent and obligation

The parent remains the full Issue #203 electron-and-neutrino mechanism, with
the electron developed first. This attempt continues P3/P5 after 0064 showed
that smooth compact-vorticity Euler interactions are dipolar and that a local
isotropic scalar source is annihilated by the accepted transverse massless
sector. It tests the materially different possibility that topology,
cohomology, or a nonlocal source map supplies signed electric charge and a
Coulomb interaction on the same substrate.

Success still requires a localized opposite-sign carrier observable, a
reciprocal `q_1 q_2/d` energy and `q_1 q_2/d^2` force, a conserved current, and
finite energy or finite excess energy on a declared physical domain. A
topological label without this action/current map is not electric charge.

## Frozen conventions

The baseline is constant-density incompressible Euler on `R^3`, with smooth
source-free fields and the whole-space Hodge/Biot--Savart recovery unless a
route explicitly declares a puncture or singular core. Differential-form
statements distinguish velocity circulation in `H^1`, vorticity flux in
`H^2`, and helicity. Distributional sources, branch cuts, external gauge
fields, inverse-Laplacian source definitions, and thermodynamic backgrounds
are recorded as additional hypotheses rather than silently treated as Euler.

## Candidate routes frozen before derivation

### Route A: point puncture and vorticity-flux cohomology

Compute the de Rham groups of `R^3` minus a point and identify the radial
closed two-form representing nonzero flux. Derive its distributional
divergence, local kinetic-energy behavior, and sign reversal. Test whether a
finite-core regularization remains source-free and preserves the cohomology
class. A positive result must include the core dynamics and reciprocal action.

### Route B: loop complement and circulation class

Use `H^1(R^3\K)` for a compact vortex loop `K`. Derive both the near-core
circulation field and the far field of a closed loop, with its energy and
multipole powers. Determine whether the topological circulation class gives a
three-dimensional scalar Coulomb monopole or only the already known vortex
dipole.

### Route C: helicity or Hopf charge as a source

Test whether helicity/Hopf degree can enter an exact Gauss law or a local
rotation-covariant transverse source. Track parity, additivity, locality, and
the dependence of helicity on the Hodge inverse. A conserved integer or real
number earns electric-charge meaning only if its variation in one joint
action produces both the mediator source and the reciprocal carrier force.

### Route D: explicitly nonlocal topological source

First classify translation-invariant linear `SO(3)`-covariant scalar-to-vector
Fourier multipliers on a declared tempered/finite-energy multiplier domain.
Then keep contact distributions at `k=0` and nonlinear or
configuration-dependent topological maps as distinct candidates, freezing
their kernel, energy, superposition, recoil, causality, and action costs. If a
construction inserts an inverse derivative, preferred direction, or new
gauge/scalar field, state that foundation input exactly. Compare this route
with the scalar/longitudinal foundation candidate retained by 0064.

## Selection and exact checks

Routes are compared by exact Euler closure, smooth localization, source
cohomology, finite energy, isotropy, signed opposites, Coulomb power,
reciprocity, and assumption cost. The analytic oracle derives the punctured
domain cohomology representatives, flux and energy powers, loop far-field
power, and Fourier covariance constraints. No production numerics or empirical
charge values are licensed.

Each route receives one route-scoped verdict. Coverage of the linear
translation-invariant multiplier class does not cover contact zero modes or
nonlinear/configuration-dependent topological maps. Failure of a frozen route
does not become a no-go for every changed substrate; it identifies the precise
extra field or singular/nonlocal input required by the next candidate.
Nothing here alone licenses charge, magnetic coupling, an electron, a
neutrino, P5, or parent completion.
