## Frozen review method

### Unit A — regular full-core cancellation and compact-edge no-go

1. Derive the full-core first-speed cancellation equation from the physical
   electric and magnetic response, with the same Green normalization and all
   density, radius, speed and electromagnetic factors.
2. Reduce it to the claimed regular radial ODE and independently obtain its
   first integral. Check origin regularity, division by the profile or radial
   derivative, sign branches, integration constants and the exact domain on
   which the derivation is reversible.
3. Prove or refute exponential uniqueness among nontrivial regular full-core
   solutions. Distinguish local uniqueness on a positive component from a
   global profile theorem across zeros.
4. Audit the compact free-edge conclusion. Verify that a nonzero exponential
   branch cannot meet the claimed smooth zero boundary at finite radius under
   the target regularity, and state every escape route the proof leaves open
   rather than converting it to a no-go for unrelated profiles or controls.

### Unit B — power-law Lane--Emden moment, sign and open-band result

5. Starting from the actual power-law Lane--Emden profile and its physical
   orientation, independently reduce the response to the claimed radial
   moment. Check convergence, signs, normalization, endpoint behavior and all
   integrations by parts.
6. Determine the exact quantifiers of any nonzero/sign theorem: power range,
   contour interval, radius scale and open band. A strict-band cutoff and its
   transition derivatives are excluded unless separately analyzed.
7. Treat the profile `P=1-s^2` only as a symbolic algebra regression or soluble
   toy limit. It cannot prove a Lane--Emden sign, exact Cao response, physical
   normalization or finite-torus theorem.
8. Check whether the theorem is pointwise, contour-averaged or paired against
   a dual functional, and keep a nonzero open-band result distinct from a
   uniform normalized lower bound.

### Unit C — fixed-leaf mean-flux obstruction to radial A/B controls

9. Reconstruct the material-leaf constraint that produces the mean-flux row in
   the straight column. Verify the measure, orientation, boundary terms,
   zero/first moments and whether radial inner/outer source controls can change
   the constrained mean.
10. Recompute the finite-torus version with the exact cylindrical Jacobian,
    curvature, inner/outer boundaries and global Green normalization. An
    asymptotic straight-column zero does not by itself prove an exact torus
    obstruction.
11. If the mean-flux functional annihilates every admissible fixed-leaf radial
    tangent while detecting the desired `A/B` target, classify the radial
    control route as refuted by that cokernel. Do not extend the mechanism to
    nonradial, multi-label, different-leaf or phase-geometry controls without a
    coverage argument.

### Unit D — finite-Cao action-coordinate material tangent

12. Freeze the finite-Cao regular-band action-angle chart, period/action
    measure, boundary regularity and coordinate orientation. Derive the exact
    weighted-zero-mean condition for a material or coadjoint tangent.
13. Prove necessity and sufficiency in the stated raw local tangent space.
    Sufficiency must construct a smooth compactly supported periodic
    divergence-free displacement in the regular band, verify `delta chi=T`,
    and give the local norm dependence on the lower bound for `|chi'(I)|` and
    the action-chart/Jacobian bounds. Circulation, impulse, center,
    tag-distribution and other finite-row restoration belong to Unit E's full
    `L_full` problem and are not required for the Unit D tangent verdict.
14. Keep this theorem strictly at tangent scope. An admissible tangent does not
    establish an exact nearby steady Cao solution, free-boundary lift,
    persistence or nonzero normalized response.

### Unit E — map to `Y_band x R^2`

15. Define the source and target Banach/Hilbert spaces, norms, regularity,
    quotient/stabilizer rows and the two finite coordinates. Verify that the
    proposed linear map is bounded and that all components share one domain.
16. Determine separately: the literal range, its closure, the annihilator or
    adjoint kernel, and whether the range is closed. A trivial adjoint kernel
    proves dense range under the stated duality, not exact surjectivity without
    a closed-range or right-inverse theorem.
17. Check whether the target is the full product `Y_band x R^2` or a compatible
    codimension subspace selected by the mean-flux row. Distinguish exact range
    from approximate controllability and finite-dimensional algebra.
18. If a Fredholm, complement or right-inverse claim is made, verify its index,
    domain, compactness and uniform bounds rather than inferring it from the
    absence of an explicit cokernel.
    Failure to construct this full operator cannot be used to reject the
    independently scoped exact results in Units A--D.

### Unit F — evidence, API and exclusions

19. Reconcile the final manifest, completion receipt, source audit, validation,
    result, derivation, verifier and importable API/tests. Preserve the static
    v2 failure as chronology and scan for stale pre-repair range or evidence
    claims.
20. Attribute exact-v2 predicates only to their encoded algebra and finite
    profiles. Focused-v2 tests are API regression; static-v3 and repository-v2
    are agreement/workflow evidence. Do not rerun unchanged evidence merely
    for counts; only a predicate defect may trigger the single bounded
    correction.
21. Preserve that no exact steady lift, finite-Cao normalized response,
    nonlinear persistence, P2/P4/P5/P6, particle, electron, neutrino or parent
    conclusion follows unless the target supplies that construction explicitly.
