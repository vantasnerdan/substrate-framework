## Frozen Varholm--Wahlén--Walsh source boundary

The primary source is K. Varholm, E. Wahlén, and S. Walsh,
[*On the stability of solitary water waves with a point
vortex*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08024v2),
`arXiv:1811.08024v2` (2019-07-29), PDF SHA-256
`16da491f9e6ed10e2decea12dc37971e04acb8494a6ddb6fd841cd44c80336bd`.
Its physical application is a two-dimensional capillary--gravity free-surface
Euler problem below a graph, with vacuum above, surface tension, gravity, and a
submerged point vortex. It is not the three-dimensional axisymmetric,
same-fluid, unbounded pure-swirl column of `0027`.

The abstract theorem is also hypothesis-rich. It assumes:

- a scale `W embedded in V embedded in X`, with `X` a real Hilbert energy
  space, reflexive Banach regularity spaces, and the stated interpolation
  inequality;
- a Hamiltonian evolution with `C3` energy and momentum, derivative
  extensions, a closed injective skew-adjoint Poisson map with dense range,
  and solution-level conservation;
- a strongly continuous affine one-parameter symmetry group that is unitary
  on `X`, isometric on `V,W`, compatible with the Poisson map, and generated by
  the momentum with the stated domain/density properties;
- a `C1` family of nondegenerate bound states `U_c`, with the exact regularity,
  domain, and orbit-separation/periodicity assumptions;
- an augmented Hessian extending to a self-adjoint energy-space operator whose
  spectrum is exactly one simple negative eigenvalue, one simple symmetry zero
  eigenvalue, and a positive remainder uniformly separated from zero;
- for conditional orbital stability, the sign `d''(c)>0` and continued
  existence with bounded well-posedness norm on the interval being considered.

The source's conditional stability conclusion can transfer only after every
one of those assumptions is proved for the actual Euler solitary wave. In
particular, an accessible traveling Hessian with high-frequency directions of
both signs fails the source's one-negative-direction hypothesis; neutral and
branch identities alone do not repair it. A mismatch is a source-applicability
result, not an instability theorem. The review will preserve any exact
Hamiltonian identities independently of whether this import fails.

Kribus--Leibovich, Szeri--Holmes, Choi, and other works listed in the `0034`
README remain motivation or candidate-method provenance unless the activated

## Exact Clebsch and physical-normalization checks

1. Fix the axisymmetric conventions `xi=r u_theta`,
   `zeta=omega_theta/r`, `dnu=r dr dz`, the poloidal streamfunction/Hodge
   inverse, bracket orientation, specific pressure, and the common physical
   multiplier `2*pi*rho_m`. Derive the Euler equations from the proposed
   Clebsch action rather than matching only a reduced scalar equation.
2. Vary every Clebsch potential and multiplier before imposing the solitary
   background. Check the symplectic one-form sign, time derivatives,
   advection equations, reconstruction of `zeta`, the swirl-source term,
   pressure/Bernoulli row, constraints, and gauge/relabeling degeneracies.
3. State the actual chart domain. A regular strip where a swirl label is
   invertible is not a global chart across a constant core, flat exterior,
   critical label, or nontrivial helicity. Track transition functions,
   closed/nonexact or harmonic rows, end data, and any distribution generated
   by a chart boundary.
4. Distinguish the full physical Euler action, an orbitwise weak action, a
   local Clebsch chart, and a quadratic linearized action. Finiteness of a
   relative observable does not make every primitive or boundary term finite.

Representative exposing changes are a reversal of the bracket or symplectic
sign, omission of `2*pi*rho_m`, loss of one Euler row after taking a curl, a
false compact-velocity assumption after Hodge projection, or deletion of a
gauge/end term.
