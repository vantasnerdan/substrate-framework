# P253/0021 — full two-contour Jacobi propagator and reduced monodromy

## Result

For every fixed García–Hassainia–Hmidi source pair and every Sobolev index
allowed by the regularity of its two boundaries, the complete labelwise
linearized contour equation has a bounded, invertible initial-value
propagator through one physical period.  Its domain contains two independent
normal perturbations, including all first modes; only tangential
reparametrization and the two exact area constraints are removed.  The
one-period map is symplectic, fixes the phase and common-translation Jacobi
fields, preserves the common energy and impulse covectors, and therefore
induces a bounded map on the correctly reduced quotient.

This is a one-period existence and norm theorem.  It does **not** put the
reduced multipliers on the unit circle, control powers of the monodromy, or
prove nonlinear orbital stability.

## 1. Actual pair, time, and contour convention

Fix the source parameters

\[
 0<a<b,\qquad \kappa>0,\qquad
 0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0,\qquad
 \lambda\in\mathcal C_\epsilon,
\]

and one actual solution supplied by source Theorem 1.1.  Put
\(L=|\log\epsilon|\), let \(\tau=Lt\), and use the physical translating-frame
period

\[
 \mathcal T=\frac{T(\epsilon,\lambda,\kappa)}{L}.
\]

In the canonical half-plane \(x=(\rho,z)\), set

\[
 J(a,b)=(-b,a),\qquad
 C_G=\frac1{\sqrt2\,\pi},\qquad
 q_*(t)=\epsilon^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^2{\bf1}_{D_{j,*}(t)}.
\]

The translating Hamiltonian density and velocity are

\[
 h_* = \Psi[q_*]-c_\epsilon\rho,
 \qquad u_*=J\nabla h_*.
\]

With each boundary oriented counterclockwise, source equation (2.10), after
\(\tau=Lt\), becomes the exact scalar geometric equation

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial_t\gamma_j\cdot J\partial_\theta\gamma_j
   =\partial_\theta h_*(\gamma_j),\qquad j=1,2.}              \tag{1}
\]

It determines normal velocity and leaves tangential parametrization free.
The stream function in (1) is the one generated by the sum of both labels;
there is no prescribed center history in this equation.

The compact periodic orbit gives the strictly positive geometric constants

\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_*&=\min_{t,j,\theta}\rho(\gamma_j(t,\theta))>0,\\
 m_*&=\min_{t,j,\theta}|\partial_\theta\gamma_j(t,\theta)|>0,\\
 d_*&=\min_{t,\theta,\eta}|\gamma_1(t,\theta)
                         -\gamma_2(t,\eta)|>0.               \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]

These are consequences of the actual smooth, disjoint source pair over a
compact period, not assumptions about independently transported copies.

## 2. Full contour chart and exact area slice

Let \(n_j\) be the outward unit normal.  Nearby unparametrized contours are
uniquely represented, after fixing a standard tangential slice, as

\[
 \Gamma_j[h_j]=\{\gamma_j(\theta)+h_j(\theta)n_j(\theta)\}.
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]

For \(s>3/2\), the tubular-neighborhood theorem and Sobolev composition make
(3) a local \(H^s\) chart modulo tangential reparametrizations.  Its exact
area map has derivative

\[
 D|D_j|_{h_j=0}[k_j]=\int_{\Gamma_j}k_j\,ds.                 \tag{4}
\]

Since (4) is nonzero on a constant normal displacement, the implicit
function theorem solves one scalar normal coordinate as a smooth function
of the zero-mean coordinates.  Thus, at each source phase, the exact
fixed-area contour manifold is locally modeled on

\[
 X_j^s(t)=\left\{k\in H^s(\Gamma_j(t)):
                  \int_{\Gamma_j(t)}k\,ds=0\right\}.         \tag{5}
\]

The constraint is individual: neither label can exchange area with the
other.

There is also an intrinsic center/shape chart, but it is a coordinate split,
not a Fourier deletion.  With \(A_j=|D_j|\), define the area centroid

\[
 C_j(D)=A_j^{-1}\int_Dx\,dx.
\]

For a normal tangent \(k_j\),

\[
 DC_j[k_j]=A_j^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_j}xk_j\,ds.                  \tag{6}
\]

For the two translation tangents \(k_j=e_\rho\cdot n_j\) and
\(k_j=e_z\cdot n_j\), the divergence theorem gives

\[
 \int_{\Gamma_j}x\otimes n_j\,ds=A_jI_2,
\]

so (6) maps them exactly to \(e_\rho,e_z\).  Hence

\[
 X_1^s(t)\oplus X_2^s(t)
 \simeq\mathbb R^4_{\rm ctr}\oplus S_1^s(t)\oplus S_2^s(t),
 \qquad S_j^s(t)=\ker DC_j\cap X_j^s(t),                     \tag{7}
\]

locally and continuously.  All physical first modes are present in the
\(\mathbb R^4_{\rm ctr}\) sector; no reversibility or relation between labels
has been imposed.

This chart agrees locally with the source radial-square variable.  From
\(\gamma=P+\epsilon wZ\) and \(w^2=1+2\epsilon f\),

\[
 \delta\gamma=\epsilon^2\frac{\delta f}{w}Z,
 \qquad
 k=\delta\gamma\cdot n
   =\epsilon^2\frac{Z\cdot n}{w}\,\delta f.                 \tag{8}
\]

Star-shapedness gives \(Z\cdot n>0\), so (8) is an invertible Sobolev
multiplication map.  Moreover the determinant-one ellipse map gives area
exactly as

\[
 |D_j|=\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}w_j^2d\theta
       =\pi\epsilon^2+\epsilon^3\int_0^{2\pi}f_jd\theta;
\]

the source zero-average condition is therefore precisely the area slice.
Applying (8) independently to both labels removes the
source's half-period and reversibility restrictions without changing the
local singular interface calculus.

Pullback by the periodic source parametrizations and the positive
multipliers in (8) gives periodic bounded isomorphisms

\[
 \mathcal J_j(t):H^s_0(\mathbb T)\longrightarrow X_j^s(t),
 \qquad H^s_0=\left\{f:\int_{\mathbb T}f\,d\theta=0\right\}.
\]

From now on, \(X^s=H^s_0\oplus H^s_0\) denotes this fixed model space.
The center/shape split (7) is transported into it.  This trivialization is
what makes the propagator and its one-period return operator act on one
fixed Banach space.

## 3. Exact independent-label linearization

Let \(\xi_j\) be a boundary variation.  Differentiating (1), before choosing
a normal gauge, gives

\[
\boxed{
 \partial_t\xi_j\cdot J\gamma_{j,\theta}
 +\partial_t\gamma_j\cdot J\xi_{j,\theta}
 =\partial_\theta\left(
      \nabla h_*(\gamma_j)\cdot\xi_j
      +\delta\Psi(\gamma_j)\right).}                         \tag{9}
\]

For outward normal displacement \(k_j=\xi_j\cdot n_j\), the domain shape
derivative has the positive sign

\[
 \delta q_j=\epsilon^{-2}k_j\,\delta_{\Gamma_j},             \tag{10}
\]

because the derivative of \(\int_{D_j}\varphi\) is
\(\int_{\Gamma_j}\varphi k_jds\).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta\Psi(x)=C_G\epsilon^{-2}
       \sum_{k=1}^2\int_{\Gamma_k(t)}G(x,y)k_k(y)\,ds_y.}    \tag{11}
\]

Equations (9)-(11) retain both self terms and both cross terms.  In a normal
graph gauge they are equivalently

\[
 (\partial_t+u_{\tau,j}\partial_s)k_j
   =(n_j\cdot\nabla u_*\,n_j)k_j
    +n_j\cdot J\nabla\delta\Psi(\gamma_j),                  \tag{12}
\]

Here the transport coefficient is fixed by the contour gauge: if
\(\partial_t\gamma_j=u_*+a_j\tau_j\), then
\(u_{\tau,j}=u_*\cdot\tau_j-a_j\).  It is zero in a material
parametrization.  A different tangential convention conjugates (12) by a
time-dependent circle diffeomorphism and leaves the geometric propagator
unchanged.  This definition fixes the sign of the transport term.

There is an independent Hamiltonian sign check.  A leaf tangent generated
by \(\chi_j\) is

\[
 \eta_j=\{q_{j,*},\chi_j\}
       =\epsilon^{-2}(J\nabla\chi_j\cdot n_j)\delta_{\Gamma_j}.
\]

Linearizing the corrected Hamilton equation
\(\dot q_j=\{q_j,h[q]\}\), with
\(i_{X_{\mathscr H_c}}\Omega=d\mathscr H_c\), yields

\[
 \partial_t\eta_j
 =\{\eta_j,h_*\}+\{q_{j,*},\delta\Psi\}.                    \tag{13}
\]

Substitution of (10)-(11) into the weak form of (13) gives (9), including
the same positive domain-variation sign in (11).  It is also the Euler-
Lagrange equation of the corrected quadratic action

\[
 \int_0^{\mathcal T}
 \left[-\frac12\Omega(\eta,\dot\eta)
       -\frac12D^2\mathscr H_c(q_*)[\eta,\eta]\right]dt.
\]

Thus the direct contour and action routes agree without changing the 0015
KKS convention.

## 4. Source/interface estimates on the full label space

The source estimates are used only at the interface where the self kernel is
singular.  Lemma 6.1 differentiates the exact contour functional into a
variable transport multiplier plus separate self and cross integral
operators.  Proposition 6.2 then decomposes the self singularity into
first-order angular transport, an order-zero Hilbert term, logarithmic
order-zero terms, and a tame infinitely smoothing remainder.  That
calculation is labelwise; applying it once to each \(\Gamma_j\) does not use
reversibility or label exchange.

For \(j\ne k\), (2) keeps \((\gamma_j,\gamma_k)\) away from the diagonal of
the Green kernel.  Define

\[
 \mathcal G_{s,*}=
 \max_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\le s+2}
 \sup_{\substack{0\le t\le\mathcal T\\
                  x\in\Gamma_1(t),\ y\in\Gamma_2(t)}}
 |\partial_x^\alpha\partial_y^\beta G(x,y)|<\infty.         \tag{14}
\]

Standard differentiation under the integral in (11) then gives the explicit
cross bound

\[
 \|K_{jk}(t)k_k\|_{H^s(\Gamma_j)}
 \le C_s\epsilon^{-2}\mathcal G_{s,*}
       \mathcal P_s(\|\gamma_1\|_{H^{s+2}},
                    \|\gamma_2\|_{H^{s+2}},m_*^{-1})
       \|k_k\|_{L^2(\Gamma_k)},                              \tag{15}
\]

where \(\mathcal P_s\) is the polynomial produced by the chain and product
rules.  Thus cross interaction is smoothing, not omitted.

After the bounded coordinate change (8), the complete two-label generator
has the form

\[
 A_*(t)k=B(t)\partial_\theta k+C(t)\mathcal H k+R(t)k,       \tag{16}
\]

where \(B,C\) are real diagonal multiplication operators, and \(R\) contains
all heterogeneous-Hilbert, logarithmic, center/shape, self-smoothing, and
the four cross operators.  No term in source Proposition 6.2 or Corollary
7.2 is discarded.  On the present fixed smooth orbit, their tame estimates
and (15) imply, for every integer \(s\) with enough source regularity,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|R(t)k\|_{H^s}&\le R_{s,*}(\epsilon,\lambda,\kappa)
                         \|k\|_{H^s},\\
 \|B(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|C(t)\|_{H^s}
   &\le B_{s,*}(\epsilon,\lambda,\kappa),                   \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]

with finite constants.  One completely explicit admissible choice is the
sum of the coefficient norms in the exact Lemma 6.1 formula, divided by its
physical-time coefficient \(\epsilon^3\), plus the right side of (15).  In
particular its gross small-core dependence is allowed to be
\(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) in the self block and
\(O(\epsilon^{-2}\mathcal G_{s,*})\) in the cross block.  No uniform-in-
\(\epsilon\) stability claim is made.

For \(\Lambda^s=(1-\partial_\theta^2)^{s/2}\), the real transport identity,
the skew-adjointness of \(\mathcal H\), and the usual commutator estimate
give

\[
 \frac12\frac d{dt}\|k(t)\|_{X^s}^2
 \le C_s(t)\|k(t)\|_{X^s}^2,                                \tag{18}
\]

where one may take

\[
 C_s(t)=C_s\left(
  1+\|\partial_\theta B(t)\|_{L^\infty}
   +\|B(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}
   +\|C(t)\|_{H^s}
   +\|R(t)\|_{H^s\to H^s}\right).                          \tag{19}
\]

All quantities in (19) are actual source-orbit coefficient norms.  In
particular

\[
 C_{s,*}:=\sup_{0\le t\le\mathcal T}C_s(t)<\infty.           \tag{20}
\]

The zero-area subspace (5) is invariant: it is the derivative of the exact
individual area conservation law.  First modes require no special estimate;
they are simply part of \(H^s\).  This is the key distinction from the
source periodic inverse.

## 5. Propagator and one-period estimate

Apply a Fourier mollifier to (16).  The regularized equations are finite-
energy ODEs, and (18) is uniform in the mollifier.  Weak compactness at
level \(H^s\), strong compactness one derivative lower, and the equation
identify a solution.  Applying (18) to a difference proves uniqueness;
solving the same equation backward proves invertibility.  Consequently

\[
 k\in C([0,\mathcal T],X^s)
      \cap C^1([0,\mathcal T],X^{s-1})                       \tag{21}
\]

and there is a two-parameter evolution family

\[
 U(t,r):X^s\to X^s,
 \quad U(t,r)U(r,a)=U(t,a),
 \quad U(r,r)=I,                                             \tag{22}
\]

with zero derivative loss and the quantitative bounds

\[
 \boxed{
 \|U(t,r)\|_{X^s\to X^s}
 \le \exp\!\left(\int_r^tC_s(\sigma)d\sigma\right)
 \le e^{C_{s,*}|t-r|}.}                                     \tag{23}
\]

The same estimate holds for \(U(r,t)=U(t,r)^{-1}\).  Periodicity of the
base coefficients gives \(U(t+\mathcal T,r+\mathcal T)=U(t,r)\), so the
actual full-contour monodromy is the bounded isomorphism

\[
 \boxed{M=U(\mathcal T,0):X^s\to X^s,
 \qquad \|M^{\pm1}\|\le e^{C_{s,*}\mathcal T}.}             \tag{24}
\]

Equation (24) is not inferred from a periodic inverse and says nothing about
\(\sup_n\|M^n\|\).

## 6. Symplectic transport and exact conserved flag

For smooth Jacobi fields \(\eta=U(t,0)\eta_0\) and
\(\zeta=U(t,0)\zeta_0\), differentiation of the KKS form and the symmetry of
\(D^2\mathscr H_c\) give

\[
 \frac d{dt}\Omega_{q_*(t)}(\eta(t),\zeta(t))=0.             \tag{25}
\]

Density extends (25) to the Sobolev tangent on which the weak KKS pairing is
continuous.  Hence

\[
 M^*\Omega_{q_*(0)}M=\Omega_{q_*(0)}.                        \tag{26}
\]

Choose the phase origin at a point where the nonconstant radial exchange has
nonzero velocity.  Then

\[
 v_{\rm ph}=\dot q_*(0),\qquad v_z=X_P(q_*(0))=-\partial_zq_*(0) \tag{27}
\]

are linearly independent.  Autonomous time translation and axial
translation give

\[
 Mv_{\rm ph}=v_{\rm ph},\qquad Mv_z=v_z.                     \tag{28}
\]

Moreover

\[
 d\mathscr H_c=\Omega(v_{\rm ph},\cdot),\qquad
 dP=\Omega(v_z,\cdot).                                      \tag{29}
\]

Thus the two covectors are independent.  Equivalently, if their boundary
densities were dependent modulo the two area constants, the trace of
\(h_*\) would be affine in \(\rho\) on both contours and (1) would make the
motion a common axial translation, contrary to the chosen nonstationary
phase.

Choose two smooth area-preserving normal bumps
\(\widehat w_1,\widehat w_2\in X^s\) for which

\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 d\mathscr H_c(\widehat w_1)&d\mathscr H_c(\widehat w_2)\\
 dP(\widehat w_1)&dP(\widehat w_2)
 \end{pmatrix}
\]

is nonsingular.  Such bumps are obtained by localizing at two boundary arcs
where the two density pairs are not collinear and subtracting an area bump
on the same label.  The normalized companions are the exact construction

\[
 \boxed{(w_E\ \ w_P)=(\widehat w_1\ \widehat w_2)A^{-1},}   \tag{30}
\]

so

\[
 d\mathscr H_c(w_E)=1,\quad dP(w_E)=0,\quad
 d\mathscr H_c(w_P)=0,\quad dP(w_P)=1.                       \tag{31}
\]

They are physical conserved-level directions, not gauge modes.  Conservation
along the nonlinear flow differentiates to

\[
 d\mathscr H_c\,M=d\mathscr H_c,\qquad dP\,M=dP.             \tag{32}
\]

Define

\[
 K=\ker d\mathscr H_c\cap\ker dP,
 \qquad G=\operatorname{span}\{v_{\rm ph},v_z\}.             \tag{33}
\]

Equations (28) and (32) give the exact invariant flag

\[
 G\subset K\subset X^s,
 \qquad M|_G=I,
 \qquad M K=K.                                               \tag{34}
\]

The companion projection onto the fixed-level tangent is

\[
 \Pi_K\eta=\eta-d\mathscr H_c(\eta)w_E-dP(\eta)w_P.          \tag{35}
\]

Choose bounded phase and axial-centering covectors
\(\ell_{\rm ph},\ell_z\) on \(K\) with
\(\ell_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij}\), and put

\[
 S=K\cap\ker\ell_{\rm ph}\cap\ker\ell_z,
 \qquad
 \Pi_Sk=k-\ell_{\rm ph}(k)v_{\rm ph}-\ell_z(k)v_z.          \tag{36}
\]

Then \(S\) is a representative of the reduced space

\[
 \mathcal N_{\rm red}=K/G,
\]

and its monodromy is

\[
 \boxed{M_{\rm red}=\Pi_SM|_S,
 \qquad
 \|M_{\rm red}\|\le\|\Pi_S\|e^{C_{s,*}\mathcal T}.}       \tag{37}
\]

In the decomposition \(X^s=S\oplus G\oplus
\operatorname{span}\{w_E,w_P\}\), the exact invariant flag forces the
block form

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 M_{\rm red}&0&B\\
 C&I_2&D\\
 0&0&I_2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                              \tag{38}
\]

This is the correct accounting of phase, translation, energy, and impulse.
The four displayed neutral/companion coordinates need not form an invariant
four-dimensional subspace: the block \(B\) can inject a conserved-level
change into reduced shape.  Removing it would require an additional
\((M_{\rm red}-I)^{-1}\) or family argument and would be circular at
multiplier one.

The exact conclusions at multiplier one are therefore:

- geometric multiplicity is at least two, from (28);
- the source filament period-twist identity predicts a phase Jordan shear,
  but a patch derivative through the Cantor family has not been proved;
- no algebraic multiplicity is assigned to the infinite-dimensional patch
  monodromy without an isolated Fredholm spectral point.

## 7. What was and was not constructed

The full non-reversible shape sector survives in (5), (12), and (24).  The
cross interaction survives in (11), (15), and the remainder of (16).  The
phase and translation directions are quotiented only after fixing both
conserved levels, while (30)-(31) retain the two physical level companions.

The source periodic inverse was not used as an evolution estimate.  The
periodic Cantor and small-divisor restrictions select the base solution; they
do not restrict the initial perturbation of the linearized IVP.

**Route A verdict — established as a bounded full-contour one-period
theorem.**  Equations (21)-(24) construct the actual independent-label
Jacobi propagator with zero Sobolev loss for a fixed sufficiently smooth
source orbit, and (33)-(38) construct its conserved and symmetry-reduced
monodromy.  The estimate is finite-period and may deteriorate as
\(\epsilon\to0\); it is not a restoring, power-bounded, or nonlinear
stability result.

**Next executable construction.**  Work on the already constructed
\(M_{\rm red}\), not on the source periodic inverse: carry the source's
symplectic normal conjugations as time-dependent IVP conjugations on the two
independent labels, retain the first-mode/center cross blocks, and derive a
reduced Krein or resolvent separation estimate strong enough to control
\(M_{\rm red}^n\).  If that separation fails at multiplier one, use the
explicit block (38) to build a modulated polynomial-growth normal form rather
than deleting the companion-induced shear.

No conclusion here concerns swirl, general three-dimensional perturbations,
helicity, intrinsic or quantum spin, an electron or neutrino identification,
parent completion, or a global no-go.
