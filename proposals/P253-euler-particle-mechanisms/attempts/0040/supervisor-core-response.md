# Accessible core support and an explicit translation response

Author: main-model supervisor, 2026-09-06. Author/unreviewed continuation of
0040 route B, supplied to foundations for the curved-ring construction.
This note derives a finite-core response rather than assuming the full
ring Feshbach estimate. It leaves all ring polarizations and the original
dynamically accessible perturbation topology in scope.

## 1. Keep exterior velocity, distinguish exterior vorticity

For a compact column W(r)e_z with support r<=a, every smooth coadjoint
tangent satisfies

    delta omega=curl(xi cross W e_z),
    supp(delta omega) subset {r<=a}.                        (C1)

If its velocities converge in kinetic L2, their curls converge as
distributions. Pairing against a test supported outside the closed core
therefore proves the same support property in the accessible closure.
Adding a graph norm cannot invalidate this implication. This is a necessary
property of that closure, not a claim that every core-supported vorticity
belongs to it. For the straight infinite column the statement can be made
fiberwise or for finite-energy axial packets.

The exterior velocity still solves the full decaying Hodge problem and has
its Bessel DtN response. In particular (C1) is not an exterior wall condition.
Linearization preserves (C1): outside the column core the base vorticity and
its derivatives vanish, and homogeneous vorticity transport cannot create
an exterior perturbation from zero initial exterior vorticity.

For each fixed nonzero cross-sectional Fourier number m, the source
vorticity equations contain multiplication by -i m Omega(r). The full
vorticity space permits passive exterior vorticity at arbitrarily small
Omega(r), whereas (C1) does not. On the core,

    Omega(r)=r^-2 int_0^r W(s)s ds >= Omega(a)>0.            (C2)

Thus the full-space essential interval ending at zero is not, by itself,
an obstruction proved for the restricted accessible problem. This argument
does not establish the essential spectrum in the kinetic/graph norm; it
identifies which proposed critical layers are absent and which operator
realization must actually be analyzed.

The equations being used are the primary full Euler column equations in
[Gallay--Smets, arXiv:1805.05064v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05064v3),
(1.13)-(1.14). Their full-enstrophy spectral description and the translation
discussion in section 3.2 are not imported as an accessible ring theorem.
The source is pinned in the existing 0040/source-audit.md.

## 2. Derive the planar m=1 core problem from time-dependent Euler

At axial wavenumber k=0, put

    v_r=i m psi/r,  v_theta=-psi',
    w=-Delta_m psi,
    Delta_m=partial_rr+r^-1 partial_r-m^2/r^2.

The actual linearized planar vorticity equation is

    w_t+i m Omega w+(i m/r) W' psi=0.                      (C3)

For time dependence exp(-i m b t), it gives the operator pencil

    T_m(b)psi=[-Delta_m+W'/(r(Omega-b))]psi=0.              (C4)

Outside the core the decaying m=1 streamfunction is proportional to r^-1,
so its exact matching condition is psi'(a)=-psi(a)/a. This is the physical
exterior condition at k=0, not an impermeable wall. The core pencil is
regular in b for |b|<Omega(a); no critical radius occurs there.

Set m=1 and

    psi0=r Omega>0.

Since W=2Omega+r Omega', direct differentiation gives

    -Delta_1 psi0=-W',  T_1(0)psi0=0.                     (C5)

Regularity gives psi0=O(r) at the axis, while W(a)=0 gives its exact exterior
Robin condition. This is the planar translation mode of Euler. Its vorticity
is generated by transverse displacement of the column, so the kernel has
the required accessible interpretation.

## 3. The finite-core complement and spectral slope are explicit

In L2((0,a),r dr), use the regular m=1 Friedrichs condition at zero and the
Robin condition above. The bounded potential W'/(r Omega) has a regular
axis limit for the smooth radial core. The quadratic form is

    t0[psi]=int_0^a (|psi'|^2+|psi|^2/r^2
                         +W' |psi|^2/(r Omega)) r dr
                 +|psi(a)|^2
           =int_0^a r psi0^2 |partial_r(psi/psi0)|^2 dr.    (C6)

The endpoint term in (C6) is essential; it cancels the outer term in the
ground-state transform. The axis term vanishes in the regular domain.
Therefore T_1(0)>=0 and its kernel is precisely span(psi0). Compact
finite-interval form embedding then gives a positive gap on its orthogonal
complement, and a bounded reduced inverse there. No uniform estimate as the
physical core shrinks is implied before nondimensionalizing the core.

Let P0 be the orthogonal projection onto psi0, Q0=I-P0. For sufficiently
small b, Q0 T_1(b) Q0 remains invertible. Its scalar Schur function is

    d(b)=<psi0,[T_1(b)-T_1(b)Q0
                (Q0 T_1(b)Q0)^-1 Q0 T_1(b)]psi0>_r.       (C7)

This convention leaves psi0 unnormalized. At b=0 the cross terms vanish,
so its derivative is exactly

    d'(0)=<psi0,T_1'(0)psi0>_r
          =int_0^a W' psi0^2/Omega^2 dr
          =int_0^a r^2 W' dr
          =-2 int_0^a r W dr=-2F(a)<0.                    (C8)

The boundary term [r^2 W]_0^a is zero. Thus the spectral slope of the actual
planar translation pencil is fixed by the core circulation, not an unknown
finite-core constant. With a unit-normalized kernel divide (C8) by
int_0^a psi0^2 r dr. Equations (C6)-(C8) construct the reduced zero-wavenumber
core response directly from dynamical Euler, not from the Cao stationary
elliptic uniqueness operator.

## 4. The actual small-k pencil and Kelvin frequency

The core response can be continued explicitly beyond k=0. Write h=Omega-b,
q=1+k^2 r^2, Phi=2Omega W. Eliminating the other velocity components from
the full three-dimensional column equations, with u_r=i psi/r, gives

    T(k,b)psi=-(1/r)partial_r[r psi'/q]
         +[1/r^2+(1/(r h))partial_r(W/q)
                         -k^2 Phi/(q h^2)]psi=0.           (C9)

For completeness the reconstruction, with gamma=i h, is

    p=(h r psi'-W psi)/q,
    u_theta=-(p+W psi)/(r h),  u_z=-i k p/gamma.            (C10)

These satisfy incompressibility and the theta/z momentum equations
identically; the radial momentum residual is -h r T(k,b)psi. Thus (C9)
is the full Euler eigen-equation in this column sector, not a fitted
streamfunction equation. It agrees with the m=1 specialization of the
source radial equation (3.2)-(3.3). Denominators h do not vanish on the
compact core when |b|<Omega(a)/2.

Outside, the velocity is grad(phi exp(i theta+i k z)), with
phi proportional to K1(|k|r). Put x=|k|a and D=x K1'(x)/K1(x).
Since psi=r phi'/i and phi solves the modified-Bessel equation, the exact
boundary condition for psi is

    psi'(a)/psi(a)=(1+x^2)/(a D).                          (C11)

The potential DtN D/a cannot be imposed directly on psi. The boundary
coefficient in the form of (C9) is instead

    -a psi'(a)/[(1+x^2)psi(a)]=-1/D
       =1+x^2[log(x/2)+gamma_E]+O(x^4 log(x)^2).           (C12)

The interior coefficients are analytic in k^2 and b near zero, with
bounded relative form perturbations on the fixed core. Treat the boundary
coefficient in (C12) as an independent small parameter. The complement gap
from (C6) gives its reduced inverse for small parameters, and the nonzero
derivative (C8) permits the ordinary scalar implicit-function argument for
the Schur function. Restoring (C12), that function has expansion

    d(k,b)=-2F(a)b+F(a)^2 k^2 log(|k|a)+O(k^2)
                         +O((|b|+k^2 |log(|k|a)|)^2).     (C13)

Here a^2 psi0(a)^2=F(a)^2 determines the logarithmic boundary coefficient.
The order-one constants in log(x/2)+gamma_E and the analytic finite-core
k^2 response enter O(k^2); no value for those constants is fitted or
asserted. The unique small root satisfies

    b(k)=-(F(a)/2) k^2 log(1/(|k|a))+O(k^2)<0.             (C14)

In particular s=-i b(k) is a nonzero imaginary column eigenfrequency, and
the core eigenfunction tends to psi0. For circulation Gamma=2pi F(a), the
leading coefficient is Gamma/(4pi). This supplies the smooth finite-core
column frequency and the core reduced inverse needed for the ring bridge;
it is still not the ring's two-polarization curvature matrix.

The exterior K1 velocity has finite radial kinetic energy for k!=0, and the
zero-k transverse translation velocity decays as r^-2. These are Fourier
fibers, not a localized three-dimensional eigenfunction of an infinite
column. The compact-support vorticity property is preserved by the matching.
To see accessibility, solve the displacement relation
(s+ad_U)xi=u on a neighborhood of the core. In cylindrical components,

    xi_r=u_r/gamma,
    xi_theta=(u_theta+r Omega' xi_r)/gamma,
    xi_z=u_z/gamma.

Its divergence vanishes by incompressibility. The Euler eigen-equation
implies delta omega=curl(xi cross W e_z), since the difference obeys the
invertible homogeneous transport/shear system there. A solenoidal extension
and cutoff outside the core leaves this curl unchanged. For the nonzero
axial fiber the same extension can be obtained by cutting off a vector
potential; the core velocity is smooth and gamma stays nonzero on a fixed
larger collar for small k. This is the accessible column mode, without
introducing passive exterior vorticity as a new degree of freedom.

## 5. The curved-ring complement remains the next construction

Curvature destroys cross-sectional m separation. In particular, the m=0
column sector has low frequencies and must be included in the ring Q block;
removing passive exterior vorticity does not remove these internal modes.
Determine the actual curvature selection rules and the m=0 resolvent before
choosing a contour or asserting a gap comparable to the bending frequency.
The positive complement gap proved in (C6) is for the planar m=1 core
pencil only, and cannot be called a gap for the complete Euler complement.

Consequently the accessible-domain simplification and exact core response
advance the original full-ring construction without substituting a smaller
perturbation class, a filament-only answer, or a planar stability conclusion.
