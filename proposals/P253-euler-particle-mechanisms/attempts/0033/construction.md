## 3. Exact physical center/shape KKS form

Use \(J(a_\rho,a_z)=(-a_z,a_\rho)\), and let

\[
 q_j=\epsilon^{-2}{\bf1}_{D_j},\qquad
 |D_j|=\pi\epsilon^2,\qquad
 \mathfrak c_j:=\int q_j\,dx=\pi .                         \tag{1}
\]

The last two identities are exact because the source profile has zero
angular mean.  A constant translation \(a=(a_\rho,a_z)\) is generated by

\[
 \chi_a(x)=a_z\rho-a_\rho z,\qquad J\nabla\chi_a=a.
\]

With the normalized product-leaf convention

\[
 \Omega_q(\delta_\chi q,\delta_\psi q)
   =-\sum_j\int q_j\{\chi_j,\psi_j\}\,dx,                  \tag{2}
\]

two translation tangents on label \(j\) obey

\[
 \Omega_j(T_a,T_b)
 =-\mathfrak c_j\det(a,b)=\pi a^TJb.                       \tag{3}
\]

Thus, in the ordered center basis
\((C_{1,\rho},C_{1,z},C_{2,\rho},C_{2,z})\),

\[
 W_{\rm ctr}=\pi\operatorname{diag}(J,J),\qquad
 W_{\rm ctr}^{\rm phys}
 =2\pi\rho_m\,\pi\operatorname{diag}(J,J).                \tag{4}
\]

This extra factor \(\pi\) is the circulation of each normalized patch.  It
is not the material factor \(2\pi\rho_m\).  Within the filament model, the
Hamiltonian expressed in these circulation-\(\pi\) center coordinates is
likewise multiplied by \(\pi\), so both factors cancel from its Hamiltonian
vector field.  This is not an assertion that the finite-core patch energy
restricts to an exact four-dimensional manifold.

The center/shape cross form is also exact.  The moment map for \(T_a\) is

\[
 \mathcal M_a(q_j)=\int\chi_aq_jdx
 =\mathfrak c_j(a_zC_{j,\rho}-a_\rho C_{j,z}),
\]

and hence

\[
 \Omega_j(T_a,k)=d\mathcal M_a[k]
 =\pi(a_z\,DC_{j,\rho}[k]-a_\rho\,DC_{j,z}[k]).             \tag{5}
\]

On the intrinsic shape slice \(S_j=\ker DC_j\), (5) vanishes.  Therefore
the KKS center--shape blocks vanish by a moment-map identity, not by an
assumption about Fourier modes.

This also resolves the source first-mode bookkeeping.  For

\[
 \gamma=P+i\epsilon L^{-1}V+\epsilon wZ,qquad
 w^2=1+2\epsilon f,
\]

a fixed-\(P\) profile tangent \(u\) satisfies

\[
 k\,ds=\epsilon^3u\,d\theta,qquad
 DC[u]=\frac{\epsilon^2}{\pi}
       \int_{\mathbb T}w(\theta)Z(\theta)u(\theta)d\theta. \tag{6}
\]

Thus source \(\cos\theta,\sin\theta\) profile directions move the physical
centroid.  At \(w=1\), an unnormalized \(\cos\theta\) direction gives
\(DC=\epsilon^2((2p_1)^{1/4},0)\), and the sine direction gives the analogous
vertical displacement.  The exact centered shape condition is

\[
 \int_{\mathbb T}wZ u\,d\theta=0,                           \tag{7}
\]

not deletion of two fixed Fourier coefficients.

Before applying (7), the fixed-\(P\) profile KKS form is exactly

\[
 \Omega_f(u,v)=-\epsilon^4\sum_{a=1}^2
 \int_{\mathbb T}(\partial_\theta^{-1}u_a)v_a\,d\theta.    \tag{8}
\]

In the normalized sine/cosine basis this reproduces

\[
 W_j^{\rm phys}=-2\pi\rho_m\frac{\epsilon^4}{j}
       \operatorname{diag}(J,J),\qquad j\ge1.              \tag{9}
\]

Equations (4)--(8) give the actual non-double-counted chart: subtract the
translation \(T_{DC[u]}\) from a fixed-\(P\) profile tangent, and retain
\(DC[u]\) as its center coordinate.  In that chart the physical KKS matrix
is the direct sum of (4) and the restriction of (8) to (7).  The
Hamiltonian/Jacobi matrix is *not* thereby block diagonal.

## 4. Exact full-label center and shape blocks

Let \({\cal C}_j k=A_j^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_j}xk\,ds\), let
\({\cal T}_ja=a\cdot n_j\), and set
\({\cal S}=I-{\cal T}{\cal C}\).  Then
\({\cal C}{\cal T}=I_4\) and
\({\cal S}X^s=S_1^s\oplus S_2^s\).  Choose a periodic bounded shape frame
\(E_S(t):S_0^s\to{\cal S}(t)X^s\), with inverse \(Q_S(t)\) on its range.
For the exact full Jacobi generator \(A_*(t)\) from the independent-label
contour equation, the moving-chart blocks are, without any projection
surrogate,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_{CC}&={\cal C}(A_*{\cal T}-\dot{\cal T}),&
 A_{CS}&={\cal C}(A_*E_S-\dot E_S),\\
 A_{SC}&=Q_S{\cal S}(A_*{\cal T}-\dot{\cal T}),&
 A_{SS}&=Q_S{\cal S}(A_*E_S-\dot E_S).                     \tag{10}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The cross-label entries in (10) are concrete source-kernel integrals.  If a
shape tangent \(k_b\) is put on label \(b\ne a\), its velocity response on
label \(a\) is

\[
 \delta u_{a\leftarrow b}(x)
 =C_G\epsilon^{-2}\int_{\Gamma_b}
       J\nabla_xG(x,y)k_b(y)\,ds_y,                         \tag{11}
\]

and its center response is

\[
 D\dot C_a[k_b]
 =\frac1{A_a}\int_{D_a}\delta u_{a\leftarrow b}(x)\,dx.   \tag{12}

For \(a=b\), (12) has in addition the exact domain-variation term

\[
 \frac1{A_a}\int_{\Gamma_a}
          (u_*(x)-\dot C_a)k_a(x)\,ds.                     \tag{13}
\]

Equations (11)--(13), together with the normal evolution

\[
 (\partial_t+u_{\tau,a}\partial_s)k_a
 =(n_a\!\cdot\nabla u_*\,n_a)k_a
  +n_a\!\cdot J\nabla\delta\Psi(\gamma_a),                \tag{14}
\]

evaluate every center--center, center--shape, and label-cross entry as an
actual integral on the published orbit.  They also show why the center
system is not closed for finite cores: (12)--(13) depend on the independent

There is a direct Hessian/connection check.  In any periodic full

Its Euler--Lagrange equation and symmetry give

\[
 A=-\bigl(W^{\rm phys}\bigr)^{-1}
       \left(L^{\rm phys}+\tfrac12\dot W^{\rm phys}\right),

\[
 \dot W^{\rm phys}+A^TW^{\rm phys}+W^{\rm phys}A=0.        \tag{14b}
\]

Thus (10)--(14) evaluate the actual low Hessian entries as well.  In the

## 5. Complete filament return and its reduction

The GHH four-dimensional Hamiltonian is independent of common \(z\), and
\(p_{1,1}+p_{2,1}=2\kappa\) is conserved.  Near a nonconstant leapfrogging
orbit, introduce the common angle/action pair \((Z,K)\) and relative
action--phase pair \((\varphi,I)\).  In a translating frame the local
filament Hamiltonian has the integrable form

\[
 h_c=h(K,I)-c_0K,
 \qquad \dot Z=\partial_Kh-c_0,
 \qquad \dot\varphi=\Omega(K,I):=\partial_Ih.               \tag{15}
\]

At the selected orbit, \(\partial_Kh=c_0\).  First use the source
\(\tau\)-time, with \(\widehat\Omega_0=2\pi/T_0\ne0\).  In the ordering
\((\delta Z,\delta\varphi,\delta K,\delta I)\), its exact return is

\[
 M_{\rm fil}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 I_2&T_0D^2_{(K,I)}h_c\\0&I_2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                             \tag{16}
\]

For the relative pair this is equivalently

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\delta\varphi(T_0)\\\delta I(T_0)\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&T_0\Omega_I\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\delta\varphi(0)\\\delta I(0)\end{pmatrix}. \tag{17}
\]

The physical return time is \(\mathcal T_0=T_0/L\), while the physical
frequencies are \(L\) times the displayed \(\tau\)-frequencies.  Their
product, and therefore the monodromy (16), is unchanged.

To check that the shear is nonzero without inferring a Hessian from
existence, set \(I=A_\sigma/(2\pi)\).  Source Proposition 3.3 uses
\(T=\partial_\sigma A_\sigma\), while at the unperturbed initial point

\[
 \sigma(\lambda)=\log\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{16\kappa},
 \qquad \sigma'(\lambda)>0.
\]

Hence \(I'(\lambda)=T\sigma'(\lambda)/(2\pi)>0\), and

\[
 \Omega_I
 =-\frac{2\pi}{T^2}\frac{T'(\lambda)}{I'(\lambda)}<0       \tag{18}
\]

for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), by the source's uniform strict twist.
This is the same Jordan identity as

\[
 (M_{\rm fil}-I)\partial_\lambda x(0;\lambda)
 =-T'(\lambda)\dot x(0;\lambda).                            \tag{19}
\]

Now apply the actual companion reduction.  On (15), axial impulse is a
nonzero multiple of \(K\), and at the orbit

\[
 d\mathscr H_c=\widehat\Omega_0\,dI,
 \qquad dP=\text{(nonzero constant)}\,dK.                  \tag{20}
\]

Therefore \(K=\ker d\mathscr H_c\cap\ker dP\) imposes
\(\delta I=\delta K=0\).  Quotienting by phase and common translation then
removes \(\delta\varphi,\delta Z\).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{(K/G)_{\rm filament}=\{0\}.}                       \tag{21}
\]

The shears in (16)--(19) live entirely between the two symmetry directions
and their energy/impulse companions.  Neither strict period twist nor a
possible drift derivative is a multiplier of the reduced shape problem.
