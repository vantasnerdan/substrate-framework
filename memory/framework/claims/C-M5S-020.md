# C-M5S-020

## Statement
The accepted statement is reproduced exactly from the claim registry.

C-M5S-020 (numeric_evidence; effective law for a frozen-superposition observable): The frozen-superposition potential cross-term of two R=24 radial fixed-J clock profiles on the common rank-1 projector background - obtained by superposing the two clocks' deviations on that background and evaluating the exactly extracted potential V(S) = -1/2 trS^2 - trS^3 + (trS^2)^2 + 0.5 in a finite cylindrical integration box (pointwise machinery matched to the functional potential integral to 8.5e-13; V(P) = 0 exactly on the projector family) - is attractive over the measured separations d = 6..28 and well fitted by a saturating exponential: Delta E(d) = 8.1133 - 5.668 * exp(-0.2442 * d) with fit ssr 6.7e-4; box/resolution refinement (2x grid: gamma = 0.271731, E_inf = 8.08826; 1.5x box: gamma = 0.265034, E_inf = 8.095008) shifts the exponent by less than 12 percent and E-infinity by less than 0.4 percent. The exponent is an effective overlap rate of the branch structure inside the box (gamma << the potential masses sqrt(3) and sqrt(5)); it is not a Yukawa mass, and odd-order classical static sourcing is excluded on the measured backgrounds by C-M5S-016. This observable is not the relaxed two-clock interaction law: the profiles are held frozen (no two-centered relaxation), only the potential telescope is evaluated (no full curvature/gradient or fixed-J energy), and the superposed clocks share one phase (no independent clock phases or two-clock inertia sector); the full two-centered construction remains the registered open objective. This frozen-superposition observable is distinct from the confined-class repulsive power law of C-M5S-008.
